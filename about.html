The Origin Story

How Fradpaix Began

It was 2018. Two mountain guides — Marco and Lena — were pinned down in a storm at the base of the Towers, waiting out three days of Patagonian wind. The kind of storm that strips everything back. In that tent they made a decision: build a guide company that actually meant something.

No mass tourism. No motorcoach itineraries. No more than eight people in the field at once. Just small teams, exceptional guides, and the honest belief that the most transformative experiences happen when you're slightly uncomfortable, deeply present, and surrounded by people who care.

Today, Fradpaix runs expeditions across 18 countries on 5 continents. The founding principles haven't changed by a single word.

What Drives Us

Our Values

01

Small is Everything

Eight people maximum per expedition. This isn't a constraint — it's the whole point. Smaller groups mean better decisions, safer terrain choices, deeper connection with place and people.

02

Safety Without Compromise

Every Fradpaix guide carries WFR certification as a minimum. We carry emergency oxygen on all high-altitude trips, run daily safety briefings, and have a clear rescue protocol for every route we operate.

03

Local Knowledge First

We don't parachute foreign guides into unfamiliar terrain. Every expedition is run with local partners who know the mountain intimately — the politics, the weather patterns, the language, the shortcuts.

04

Leave No Trace

We operate under strict LNT principles. All human waste is packed out on remote routes. We offset 120% of all expedition carbon. We contribute 1% of revenue to conservation projects in each region we operate.

05

Radical Honesty

If a route is too difficult for your current fitness level, we'll tell you. If weather makes a summit bid unsafe, we descend. We'd rather disappoint you once than put you at risk — and we've never lost that principle.

06

Genuine Connection

The best part of every expedition isn't the summit. It's the dinner conversation at basecamp on day five, the shared exhaustion, the moment someone says "I didn't know I could do that." We build space for that.
