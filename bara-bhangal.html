About the Trek

The Bara Bhangal trek is one of the most demanding and revered high-altitude treks in Himachal Pradesh, connecting the Kullu Valley to the Kangra Valley through remote, rugged terrain. It typically starts from the Kullu side (often via Manali or the Solang/Lamadugh area) and crosses two formidable passes — Kalihani Pass (~4,800m) and Thamsar Pass (~4,700m) — before descending into the isolated village of Bara Bhangal, one of the most cut-off settlements in the Himalayas, still reliant on traditional shepherd life and only accessible on foot for much of the year.

From there, the route continues toward Kangra, often exiting via Billing or Rajgundha, famous as a paragliding launch site.

The trek spans roughly 8–11 days and covers dramatic landscapes: dense forests, high alpine meadows, glacial moraines, and river crossings, with constantly shifting weather that can turn harsh even in summer.

It's rated difficult to very difficult, recommended only for experienced trekkers with prior high-altitude experience, given the technical pass crossings, unpredictable snow conditions, and complete lack of cell connectivity or rescue infrastructure along most of the route.

The best season is typically mid-July to early September, right after the passes clear of heavy snow but before winter sets in early at that altitude. Given its remoteness and difficulty, this trek is usually done with experienced local guides and porters, and proper acclimatisation is critical, since altitude sickness risk is high throughout.

12-Day Detailed Itinerary

Bara Bhangal Trek starts from Manali. We gather in Manali, obtain all necessary licenses and permits, and start early in the morning. The road to Lama Dugh is covered with thick forests offering views of Deo Tibba, Indrasan, and Bara Shigri glaciers. Constant steep slopes lead to the ridge and finally Lama Dugh campsite. Night stay in tents.

One water point at school on the way — carry 2–3 litres.

One of the most exciting days. Start early after light breakfast. Walk through sloppy meadow areas with beautiful views. Pass Hanuman Tibba and Dhauladhar Tibba peaks. After ~2 hours reach Riyali Thatch — an amazing place with shepherd huts. Spend the night here discussing the next day's itinerary.

Carry enough water for 3 hours.

Start after light breakfast. The trail is full of steep climbs, gushing streams, bushes, and trees through open meadows. Pass Rani Sui Lake, cross a stream, then continue on a steep 3-hour climb. At Kalihani Base Camp you'll be rewarded with glimpses of Indrasan and Deo Tibba peaks. Overnight in tents.

Carry enough water for 3 hours.

A long, demanding day — start early after breakfast. Walk on steep climbs revealing elegant views of the Dhauladhar Ranges and Pir Panjal. Alluring glacial lakes and glaciers line the route. After the steep slope, reach the campsite. Overnight in tents.

Very few water points — carry enough for the full day.

Difficult day through dense forests of Oak and Fir with several ups and downs on a downward trail. The most challenging part is the steep incline before Dev Ki Marhi. Reach campsite around noon — enjoy tea and snacks before spending the night in tents amid picturesque landscapes.

Start after a healthy breakfast. Climb upwards towards Dal Marhi through forests, rivers, and the Kalihani stream. Amazing flora and fauna with constant ups and downs. A vertical steep climb at the end makes you exhausted — but the splendid surroundings of Dal Marhi make it worth every step. Dinner and night stay in tents.

Few water points — carry enough for the day.

Travel downward through deodar forests along the River Ravi. Pass through pine, birch, and rhododendron forests on a steep descent into Bara Bhangal — a small, remote hamlet with vast fields of corn and rajma, a satellite phone, and a hydel power project.

Carry water for more than 6 hours.

Take rest at the campsite in Bara Bhangal. The unique weather conditions need time to adjust to. Explore local sights, discover the tradition and culture, photograph the stunning landscapes, and let the surreal nature of this isolated hamlet refresh you for the journey ahead.

Fresh after rest, start after breakfast. Cross Thamsar Pass and walk for ~6 hours. After the pass, enter a heaven of pine and birch forests. After reaching Mardh, enjoy tea and snacks. Fun activities and a tasty dinner will remove the day's tiredness. Overnight in tents.

Many water points on this route.

Difficult but interesting — travel ~8 hours capturing panoramic views of Kangra Valley. Cross moraines and glaciers to reach Palachak. A small lake appears soon after the moraines. Lush green Kangra Valley, forests, streams, and huge mountains reconnect you to yourself. Night in tents at Palachak.

Only one lake on this road — carry enough water.

One of the easiest days. Flat route traversing pine trees and Kangra Valley. Vehicle arranged from Billing to drop at Bir — one of the most elegant spots in Himachal, famous for paragliding and breathtaking beauty.

Many streams available for water.

Leave Bir after breakfast. Choose to stay and explore, or pick up a flight back to your city. You can spend some hours discovering alluring Bir-Billing and its hidden spots.
